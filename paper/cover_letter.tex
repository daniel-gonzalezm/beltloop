\begin{letter}{Editor-in-Chief\\Journal of Sound and Vibration}
\opening{Dear Editor,}

I am submitting the manuscript \emph{Closed-form longitudinal modal analysis of belt
conveyors with a gravity take-up at an arbitrary position} for consideration as a research
article in the \emph{Journal of Sound and Vibration}, within the scope of analytical
methods and modelling of linear vibration.

The gravity take-up is a counterweight hanging on a pulley wrapped by the return belt, and
its position on long overland conveyors is chosen conveyor by conveyor, by simulation or
experience. The manuscript models the whole belt loop as a Kelvin--Voigt continuum with two
wave speeds, a speed-controlled drive anywhere on the loop and the take-up anywhere on the
return strand, represented as a pulley with mass, and derives its characteristic equation in
closed form with transfer matrices. Its main results are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item a light take-up acts as a free end: the loop splits into two strands, fixed at the
        drive and free at the take-up, whose modes interlace with those of the loop. Finite-element
        simulations and field records had shown this case by case; no previous model builds it
        into its structure;
  \item the take-up mass enters as a tip-mass correction in closed form, and since a gravity
        take-up's mass follows from its tension, it lengthens the fundamental period of the
        published conveyors longer than \qty{1}{\kilo\metre} by at most
        \qty{\NumLongShiftMax}{\percent};
  \item the take-up position sets the lengths of the two strands, so moving it from head to
        tail shortens the fundamental period by up to a factor of two (equal wave speeds);
  \item each strand starts up along a universal curve of the fixed--free string, which gives
        the drive tensions, the take-up travel and the drive grip condition in closed form.
\end{itemize}
The solution is verified against an independent lumped-mass model and is checked against
take-up records and wave-speed measurements of field conveyors; the physical validation is
partial, and the manuscript states so.

The manuscript is original, has not been published, and is not under consideration
elsewhere. I am the sole author and have no competing interests to declare. The code that
produces every figure, table and number of the article is openly available under the MIT
licence and archived on Zenodo, as described in the data availability statement.
I have chosen to post the manuscript as a preprint on SSRN through the journal's preprint
service.

\closing{Yours sincerely,}
