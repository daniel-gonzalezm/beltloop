\opening{Dear Editor,}

I am submitting the manuscript \emph{Closed-form longitudinal modal analysis of belt
conveyors with a gravity take-up at an arbitrary position} for consideration as a research
article in \emph{Applied Mathematical Modelling}.

Long overland belt conveyors in mining are tensioned by a gravity take-up, a counterweight
hanging on a pulley wrapped by the return belt, and its position shapes the transient tensions
of every start and stop. Practice chooses that position conveyor by conveyor, by simulation or
experience. The manuscript gives a closed-form model for this engineering decision: the belt
loop is modelled as a Kelvin--Voigt continuum with two wave speeds, a speed-controlled drive
anywhere on the loop and the take-up anywhere on the return strand, represented as a pulley
with mass, and transfer matrices give its characteristic equation in closed form. Its main
results are:
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}
  \item the free end that a light take-up is known to form holds at any position; moving the
        take-up from head to tail shortens the fundamental period by a factor
        \NumHeadTailRatioGammaThree--\NumHeadTailRatioGammaOneFive\ on loaded belts, so it is not
        the equivalent transformation it has been taken for;
  \item the take-up mass enters as a closed-form tip-mass correction, and since it follows from
        the take-up tension it lengthens the fundamental period of the published conveyors of
        \qty{1}{\kilo\metre} or longer by at most \qty{\NumLongShiftMax}{\percent};
  \item a universal start-up curve gives the drive tensions, the take-up travel, the drive grip
        condition and a start-time rule; the published \qty{30}{\second} starts of a
        \qty{1}{\kilo\metre} conveyor would have slipped on its single drive pulley.
\end{itemize}
The tools are classical; what is new is their use with the take-up and drive positions as
continuous parameters. The solution is verified against a second
implementation and checked against take-up records and wave-speed measurements of field
conveyors; the physical validation is partial, and the manuscript states so. The code that
produces every figure, table and number is openly available and archived on Zenodo.

An earlier version was submitted to the \emph{Journal of Sound and Vibration} (JSV-D-26-03301)
and declined without review as outside its scope, the editor suggesting a journal oriented to
applications in mechanical or mining engineering; no technical objections were raised. The
manuscript has since been shortened to the length of this journal and reframed around the
engineering problem, with supporting details in appendices and in a supplementary file.

The manuscript is original, has not been published, and is not under consideration
elsewhere. I am the sole author and have no competing interests to declare.
