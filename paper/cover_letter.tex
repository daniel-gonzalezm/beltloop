\begin{letter}{Editor\\Mechanism and Machine Theory}
\opening{Dear Editor,}

I am submitting the manuscript \emph{Effect of the gravity take-up position on the longitudinal
dynamics of long belt conveyors} for consideration as an original research paper in
\emph{Mechanism and Machine Theory}.

Long overland belt conveyors are tensioned by a gravity take-up, a counterweight hanging on a
pulley wrapped by the return belt, and where the take-up sits along the loop shapes the
transient tensions of every start and stop. Designers choose that position conveyor by conveyor,
by simulation, by experience or through the static tensions. The manuscript answers the design
question in closed form: the belt loop is a Kelvin--Voigt continuum with two wave speeds, a
speed-controlled drive anywhere on it and the take-up, a pulley with mass, anywhere on the
return strand. For the designer it gives:
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}
  \item which strand vibrates and its fundamental period as a function of the take-up position:
        moving the take-up from head to tail shortens that period by a factor
        \NumHeadTailRatioGammaThree--\NumHeadTailRatioGammaOneFive\ on loaded belts, so it is
        not a neutral design change;
  \item when the take-up mass matters: set by the take-up tension, it lengthens the fundamental
        period of the published conveyors of \qty{1}{\kilo\metre} or longer by at most
        \qty{\NumLongShiftMax}{\percent};
  \item the start-up design quantities, from a universal start-up curve: the drive tensions, the
        take-up travel, the drive grip and a start-time rule of 2--3 fundamental periods; the
        published \qty{30}{\second} starts of a \qty{1}{\kilo\metre} conveyor would have slipped
        on its single drive pulley.
\end{itemize}
The work falls within the dynamics of machinery and the transportation machinery named in the
scope of the journal, and it connects with work published here on belt conveyor drives, belt
friction before gross slip and axially accelerating media, which the manuscript cites. The tools
are classical; what is new is their use on the whole loop with the take-up and drive positions
as continuous design parameters. The solution is verified against a second implementation and
checked against take-up records and wave-speed measurements of field conveyors; the physical
validation is partial, and the manuscript states so. The code that produces every figure, table
and number is openly available and archived on Zenodo.

The manuscript is original, has not been published, and is not under consideration
elsewhere. I am the sole author and have no competing interests to declare.
