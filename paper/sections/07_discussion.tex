\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Relation to practice and to previous models}\label{sec:relation}

The strand picture gives a mechanical content to the reasons of practice quoted in
Section~\ref{sec:intro}. A take-up right after the drive keeps the exit tension constant
\citep{lodewijks2013} because strand~A, between the drive exit and the free end, is then short
and its dynamic tension $D(t_a/\tper{A1})\,m_Aa_m$ vanishes with $\xi$. A tail take-up makes the
whole return strand strand~A, which the drive exit must set in motion from the start: hence the
concern that the take-up ``has to move the entire return strand'', so that the start must be long
or the take-up tension high \citep{barfoot1997}. The warning that a slack-side gravity take-up
may behave violently unless starts and stops are long \citep{harrison1989} is quantified by the
travel curve, which overshoots most at about one fundamental period. Changing the counterweight
at fixed rigging, as \citet{gao2026} did, also changes the pre-tension
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:beta_tension}); they attribute the resulting changes of tension to it themselves,
and since the inertia alone would change the fundamental period of their conveyor by
\qty{\NumShiftG}{\percent}, their changes of displacement and velocity most likely have the same
origin. Slip at the drive is observed in the field \citep{kulinowski2013,kulinowski2021}.

Devices that resist the carriage move the take-up away from the free end
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:transmission_z}): a hydraulic buffer transmits part of every front, a rigid stop
makes the take-up a fixed pulley, with the spectrum $(\mat B\mat A)_{12}=0$, and a retarder keeps
it transparent until its drag is exceeded \citep{morrison1987,nordell1987}. The free-end results
hold while the carriage moves freely.

\subsection{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}

The take-up is frictionless and its rig has no inertia (A7), whereas measured carriage forces
fluctuate with the inertia of the weights and the efficiency of the rig
\citep{kulinowski2021,poe2000sheave}, and measured carriage oscillations die out within one or
two cycles \citep[Fig.~9]{kulinowski2013}; such friction damps the oscillations after a
speed-controlled start more than its peak tensions. Contact of the carriage with its
stops \citep{kung2005henderson,zamorano1991} lies outside the linear model, which instead sizes
the travel that the tower must allow. Sag at low tension \citep{ellis1987} and the dependence of
the modulus on tension and temperature \citep{zarzycki2023,hou2008} change the wave speeds, and
with them every period in seconds, but not the dimensionless results. The resistances appear
along the whole loop at once rather than behind the stretching front. Drives on fluid couplings
or with stepped torque change the spectrum and need not reflect the waves (\cref{app:torque};
\citet{morrison1987}). The layout has one drive station, homogeneous strands and a straight
path. Finally, none of the field checks tests the take-up mass: a start record with a free
take-up, a speed-controlled drive, a time history of the take-up and published conveyor data
would test the model directly, and modern overland conveyors already record the take-up
position in operation \citep{kruse2019overland}.
