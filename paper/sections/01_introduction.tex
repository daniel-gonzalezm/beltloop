\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Overland belt conveyors carry ore and coal over several kilometres, and over such lengths the
belt is an elastic system: a change of drive speed travels along it as a stress wave, more
slowly on the loaded carry strand than on the return strand, so the belt far from the drive
starts moving seconds after the belt at the drive \citep{harrison1983,nordell1984,lodewijks2002}.
The transient tensions govern the belt rating, the take-up travel and the grip on the drive
pulleys, and dynamic analysis is part of the design of long conveyors
\citep{lodewijks2002,nordell1997zisco}. Most are tensioned by a gravity take-up: a counterweight
hung from a carriage whose pulley is wrapped by the return belt, which holds the slack-side
tension and stores the belt that the loop releases or takes up as its stretch changes.

Where to place the take-up is decided partly on dynamic grounds, but one conveyor at a time. A
take-up right after a head drive keeps the tension leaving the drive constant
\citep{lodewijks2013}; a take-up at the tail, or at the lowest tension, was recommended against
the surge of the return belt at start-up \citep{harrison1985b}; at ZISCO the take-up went to the
tail because a head take-up would have caused larger tension fluctuations in a horizontal curve
\citep{nordell1997zisco}; and a simulated stop left very different minimum tensions with the
take-up at the head and at the tail \citep{cdi2002}. Owners have moved take-ups ``in the hope of reducing observed hazards''
\citep{nordell1987}, and the location is said to be dictated by the terrain and ``by the belt
dynamic behavior'' \citep{cornet2002}. The one study devoted to the question compared a head
and a tail take-up through the static tensions, called the dynamics vital but left them out,
and concluded that each conveyor needs evaluation in its own right \citep{harrison1989}.

The two wave speeds of the loop are well established \citep{nordell1984,lodewijks2002,cdi1998}.
Discrete and finite-element models include the kinematics of the take-up pulley, which stores
twice its own travel of belt \citep{lodewijks1996,kulinowski2014,song2003}, but answer the
question one layout at a time; equivalent models embed the take-up in the belt as a mass
\citep{harrison1983,sakharwade2019}, which gives the wrong limits
(Section~\ref{sec:interfaces}). Among continuum models, \citet{song2012} wrote the two-strand
continuum with the take-up condition, neglected the take-up inertia and moved the take-up from
the head drive to the tail, a move they describe as an equivalent transformation;
\citet{zhang1999}, \citet{pang2015} and \citet{li2018} treated a tail take-up as a mass that
moves with the end of the belt; others leave out the return strand
\citep{ellis1987,pihnastyi2020}; and in a recent discrete study the counterweight mass was
varied together with the static tension it produces \citep{gao2026}.

The mechanism that links the take-up to the loop is also known: a take-up whose inertial
impedance is small against that of the belt holds a constant force and acts as a free end, which
splits the loop into two strands, each fixed at the drive and free at the take-up. Field records
showed the return belt beyond the take-up at rest until the stress had gone round the
loop \citep{harrison1985b}, simulations showed the reflection \citep{morrison1987,nordell1987},
\citet{harrison1983} estimated the slow take-up oscillation as a belt spring with the take-up
at its end, and \citet{song2012} used the free ends to separate the two strands of their
continuum, with the take-up at the tail. What has not been worked out is what the mechanism implies when the
take-up and the drive can sit anywhere: how the take-up position sets the spectrum and the
start-up tensions, and when the take-up mass stops being negligible.

The tools needed are classical: transfer matrices, the interlacing of receptances, Rayleigh's
quotient with a tip mass and d'Alembert's solution of a fixed--free string. What this paper
adds is their use on the whole loop, with the take-up and drive positions as continuous
parameters, which gives:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item a closed-form characteristic equation for the loop with two wave speeds, a gravity
  take-up modelled as a pulley with mass anywhere on the return strand and the drive anywhere
  on the loop, whose split into two fixed--free strands holds for a light take-up at any
  position (Section~\ref{sec:solution});
  \item the take-up mass as a tip mass of a quarter of its value, and the link
  $\beta=(4/\nstr)\lambda\Tt/\Wret$ between mass ratio and take-up tension, which bounds its
  effect on the fundamental period of real conveyors (Section~\ref{sec:betaregime});
  \item bounds on the fundamental period as a function of the take-up position, which show that
  moving the take-up to the tail is not an equivalent transformation
  (Section~\ref{sec:frequencies});
  \item a universal start-up curve of the fixed--free strand, a finite d'Alembert sum, for the
  drive tensions, the take-up travel, the grip condition and a start-time rule
  (Sections~\ref{sec:startup} and~\ref{sec:validity}).
\end{enumerate}
The results are verified against a second implementation, checked against partial field records
(Section~\ref{sec:validation}) and applied to three published conveyors
(Section~\ref{sec:application}).
