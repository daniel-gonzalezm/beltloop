In the free-end limit the spectrum of the loop is the union of those of strands A and~B, so the
modes are followed by strand: B1, B2,~\dots\ are born at the fixed--free frequencies of
strand~B, and A1, A2,~\dots\ at those of strand~A. \Cref{fig:modal} maps the fundamental in the
plane $(\xi,\gamma)$, the two parameters left when $\beta\to0$.

\paragraph{The fundamental belongs to strand B} With a head drive and $\gamma\ge1$, strand~B
holds the fundamental for every take-up position: in Eq.~\eqref{eq:strandB} both tangents grow
from zero on $0<\Omega<\pi/(2\gamma)$, so the product crosses $\gamma$ once there, whereas
strand~A starts at $\pi/(2\xi)\ge\pi/(2\gamma)$. At $\Omega=\pi/(2\tauB)$, where
$\tauB=1-\xi+\gamma$ is the transit time of strand~B, the product equals $1\le\gamma$, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bounds_head}
  4\gamma\le\tauper{1}\le4\tauB ,
\end{equation}
with equality on both sides for a take-up at the tail, where strand~B is the carry strand
alone, and on the right for $\gamma=1$. Over $1\le\gamma\le3$ the rule of four transits
overestimates the period by up to \qty{\NumModalFourTransitOver}{\percent}
(\cref{fig:modal}b), because the light return belt at the free end raises the frequency of
strand~B. Moving the take-up changes the strand that holds the fundamental, and with it the
period (\cref{fig:modal}a): from the head to the tail the period falls by a factor
\NumHeadTailRatioGammaOneFive, \NumHeadTailRatioGammaTwo\ and \NumHeadTailRatioGammaThree\ for
$\gamma=1.5$, 2 and 3, the range of loaded belts, and by \NumHeadTailRatioGammaOne\ for
$\gamma=1$, an empty belt. The move to the tail that \citet{song2012} made as an equivalent
transformation changes the fundamental period of their conveyor from
\qty{\NumSongPeriodHeadLight}{\second} at the head drive of their Fig.~1 to
\qty{\NumSongPeriodTailLight}{\second} (take-up as a free end; \cref{tab:cases} gives both with
the mass). A drive on the return strand adds a run of return belt at the fixed end of strand~B,
which acts as a soft spring and lengthens the period beyond four transits (\cref{fig:modal}d;
supplementary material, S3).

\paragraph{Modal participation} A uniform fixed--free string has $8/\pi^2$ of its mass in its
fundamental; applied to strand~B, of mass $m_B=\mu_rL\,(1-\xi+\gamma^2)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:F1}
  \frac{\Gamma_1^2m_1}{1+\gamma^2}\simeq\frac{8}{\pi^2}\,\frac{1-\xi+\gamma^2}{1+\gamma^2},
\end{equation}
exact for $\gamma=1$ and at most \qty{\NumModalParticipationFormulaErr}{\percent} too high
otherwise. The fraction falls as the take-up moves towards the tail (\cref{fig:modal}c), and
A1 carries exactly $(8/\pi^2)\,\xi/(1+\gamma^2)$.

\subsection{When the take-up mass matters}\label{sec:betaregime}

\paragraph{The threshold} Let $\Omega_p$ be a fixed--free frequency of one strand and
$\Omega_t=\Omega_p/1.05$. Since~\eqref{eq:receptance} is linear in $1/\beta$, the take-up mass
that lengthens the period of that mode by \qty{5}{\percent} is explicit,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:beta5}
  \betafive=\frac{4}{\Omega_t^2\,\bigl[H_A(\Omega_t)+H_B(\Omega_t)\bigr]},
\end{equation}
provided no strand frequency lies in $[\Omega_t,\Omega_p)$ (a frequency just above $\Omega_p$
also lowers it, \cref{fig:beta}b); for an isolated pole, $\betafive\simeq\NumSinglePoleCoefficient\,\tilde m$
by~\eqref{eq:onepole}. For the fundamental (\cref{fig:beta}a), $\betafive$ depends mainly on
$\gamma$: the heavier the carry strand, the larger the mass that strand~B carries at its free
end. For $\gamma\ge1.4$ and
$\xi\le0.1$, $\betafive\ge\NumBetaFiveMinNearDrive$. The participation (\cref{fig:modal}c,
hatched) and the mode A1 (S5) have lower thresholds.

\paragraph{Where the conveyors lie} By Eq.~\eqref{eq:beta_tension}, the mass ratio follows from
the take-up tension and factorises as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:beta_factors}
  \beta=\frac{4\lambda}{\nstr}\,\frac{F_U}{\Wret}\,\frac{\Tt}{F_U},
\end{equation}
with $F_U$ the running peripheral force. For given slope, load and resistance coefficients
neither factor depends on the length, because the take-up tension is sized by requirements that
grow with it, chiefly drive grip \citep[Eqs.~47--50]{din22101} and the weight of the return
strand on a slope: the mass ratio does not decrease as the conveyor gets longer. For the
conveyors of \cref{tab:cases} of \qty{1}{\kilo\metre} or longer, $\Tt/\Wret$ is
\NumLongTtWrMin\ to \NumLongTtWrMax\ and $\beta/\betafive$ of the fundamental is
\NumLongBetaRatioMin\ (G) to \NumLongBetaRatioMax: the take-up mass lengthens their fundamental
period by at most \qty{\NumLongShiftMax}{\percent} (S with the take-up at the tail, and Pa), and
by at most \qty{\NumLongShiftMaxLow}{\percent} over the coupling range of each material (Pa). With
a counterweight hung directly on two strands, $\beta=2\Tt/\Wret$, so for a take-up near the drive
and $\gamma\ge1.4$ the take-up mass changes the fundamental period by less than
\qty{5}{\percent} as long as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rule}
  \Tt\lesssim\frac{\Wret}{4}.
\end{equation}
This sufficient condition for the fundamental period makes a conveyor long in a dynamic sense,
a notion without a strict definition even for the route length \citep{suchorab2025longdistance};
otherwise the test is $\beta\ll\betafive$ with Eq.~\eqref{eq:beta5}. In short conveyors sag and holdback set the take-up tension and $\beta$
grows as $1/L$; among the published conveyors only one of \qty{\NumWRCLength}{\metre} lies
clearly beyond its threshold (S5). None of the field records of Section~\ref{sec:validation}
can therefore test the take-up mass.

With the take-up as a free end, the drive-entry tension depends only on strand~B and the
drive-exit tension only on strand~A, each a fixed--free string driven at its fixed end. For a
uniform strand the peak tension at the fixed end over the inertial force $m_sa_m$ depends on
$\tau_a/\tauper{s}$ alone: this amplification $D(\tau_a/\tauper{s})$ is the universal curve of
\cref{fig:startup}a. For a slow start the strand moves as a rigid body and $D\to1$; for a fast
one, until the wave returns from the free end after half a period, the tension follows the drive
velocity, $T=Z_sV(t)$ with $Z_s=\mu_sc_s$ the impedance of the strand
\citep[Art.~284]{love1906}. Since a wave changes sign on reflection at the free end and keeps
it at the driven end, the fixed-end tension and the free-end displacement are finite sums,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dalembert}
\begin{gathered}
  T(0,t)=Z_s\Bigl[V(t)+2\sum_{k\ge1}(-1)^kV\bigl(t-\tfrac{k}{2}\tper{s}\bigr)\Bigr],\\
  u(\ell_s,t)=2\sum_{k\ge0}(-1)^kU\bigl(t-\tfrac{2k+1}{4}\tper{s}\bigr),
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
with $V=U=0$ for negative arguments. The universal curve $D$ is the extreme of the first over
$m_sa_m$, and the travel curve $D_y$ (\cref{fig:startup}d) that of the second over the
quasi-static elongation $a_m\ell_s^2/(2c_s^2)$. The sine curve peaks at \NumStartPeakMax\ for
$\tau_a/\tauper{s}=\NumStartPeakAt$ and decays as $\tauper{s}/\tau_a$, because the sine
acceleration starts and ends with a jump in jerk.
% tests: test_metrics.py (wave law, exact for tau_a <= T_s/2; peak 1.590 at 0.87; D(1), D(2), D(5))

\paragraph{Collapse of the loop} Applied to the two strands, the curve gives in closed form
the extreme dynamic tensions at the two faces of the drive and the take-up travel,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:startup_formulas}
\begin{gathered}
  \frac{\Delta T_\mathrm{entry}}{m_Ba_m}\simeq D\!\left(\frac{t_a}{\tper{1}}\right),\qquad
  \frac{\Delta T_\mathrm{exit}}{m_Aa_m}\simeq -D\!\left(\frac{t_a}{\tper{A1}}\right),\\
  \frac{y_\mathrm{max}}{a_mL^2/c_r^2}\simeq\frac12\left[D_y\!\left(\frac{t_a}{\tper{1}}\right)I_B-I_A\right],
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
where $m_A=\mu_r\xi L$, $\tper{A1}=4\xi L/c_r$, and $I_B=\int_\xi^2\int_\xi^x\hat\mu\,\dd x'\,\dd x$ and $I_A=\xi^2/2$ are the
quasi-static elongations of the strands. Strand~B is not uniform when $\gamma\ne1$, but its
fundamental dominates (Eq.~\eqref{eq:F1}), and the loop stays within
\qty{\NumCollapseEntryEight}{\percent} of the curve at the entry for $t_a\ge0.8\,\tper{1}$
(\cref{fig:startup}c).
% tests: test_metrics.py::test_travel_from_the_strands (rel 0.06)

\paragraph{Design rule} Since the amplification depends on $t_a/\tper{1}$ only, the start time
needed for a given amplification is proportional to the fundamental period. A start of two to
three periods keeps the inertial amplification at \NumStartDThree--\NumStartDTwo\ and that of
resistances that build up with the belt speed at most \NumStartResTwo; we take
$t_a\gtrsim2$--$3\,\tper{1}$ as the rule, a choice of acceptable amplification rather than a
strength criterion: for the conveyor of
\citet{lodewijks1996} it gives \NumLoRuleMin--\qty{\NumLoRuleMax}{\second} at full coupling of the material (from
\qty{\NumLoRuleMinLow}{\second} at the weakest), against
\qty{60}{\second} by the rule of one minute per kilometre and \qty{56.1}{\second} by his strength
criterion, both smooth in his simulations \citep[Table~8.8]{lodewijks1996}, whereas grip
requires a longer start (Section~\ref{sec:application}). Published start times span the whole
curve (\cref{fig:startup}a).

\subsection{Validity: slack, take-up and drive grip}\label{sec:validity}
% Full derivation, figure and accuracy checks in Appendix D. Tests: test_validity.py.

With the strand curves, the admissibility conditions of Section~\ref{sec:modal} become explicit
lower bounds on the running tension $T_2$ at the drive exit (\cref{app:validity}, for
resistances $r=\rho\,\mu\,a_m$). \emph{Slack}: the exit tension drops during the start by the
dynamic tension of strand~A, small for a take-up near the drive, and strand~B rebounds after the
start, unless $t_a=(j+\tfrac12)\,\tper{1}$ or running resistances $\rho\ge1$ hold it; the carriage
follows the belt with a wide margin. \emph{Drive grip}: adding the peaks of the two strands,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:grip}
  T_2\ge\frac{m_Ba_m\,C_\mathrm{max}(t_a/\tper{1})+\egrip\,m_Aa_m\,C_\mathrm{max}(t_a/\tper{A1})}
  {\egrip-1}-\rho\,m_Aa_m ,
\end{equation}
where $C_\mathrm{max}$ is the universal curve with resistances ($C_\mathrm{max}=D$ for $\rho=0$).
For slow starts~\eqref{eq:grip} reduces to the running condition $T_2\ge F_U/(\egrip-1)$ of
DIN~22101, whose start-up factor, at most 1.7 for large conveyors, is fixed in advance
\citep[\S7.3.1]{din22101}; here it follows from the start time, through $t_a/\tper{1}$ and $\rho$. With a single drive pulley
($\egrip\approx3$) grip governs over slack for every take-up position; with a tandem drive
($\egrip\approx16$) slack, rebound and grip are comparable.

\subsection{Application to three field conveyors}\label{sec:application}
% Full account, figure and table in the supplementary material (S2).
% Numbers: paper_numbers.py, section "Application to field conveyors".
% Tests: test_applications.py, test_tables.py.

Three conveyors of \cref{tab:cases} were started in physical units with the full model, DIN~22101
resistances fitted to the running force and building up with the belt speed, and the take-up
moved along the return strand with the case's counterweight (supplementary material, S2). The
starts are hypothetical speed-controlled sine starts on real layouts: the base case of Lodewijks is a linear ramp with an offset, of the same duration; Surtees' conveyor has a fluid
coupling, and for the incline of Suchorab-Matuszewska et al., whose start is not published,
$3\tper{1}=\qty{\NumSMReferenceStart}{\second}$ is assumed.

The conveyor of \citet{lodewijks1996} has a single drive pulley ($\egrip=\NumLoGripFactor$)
and a take-up tension of \qty{\NumLoTt}{\kilo\newton}, sized for a running force of
\qty{35.6}{\kilo\newton} \citep[Eq.~8.6]{lodewijks1996} with the start-up factor of a fluid
coupling. A rigid belt with the same loads already needs
\qty{\NumLoTtReqRigid}{\kilo\newton} to stay in grip during a sine start of \qty{30}{\second},
the duration of his starts, so these would have slipped, which his simulations, without slip at
the drive, could not show; on a rigid belt the installed tension holds grip only in sine starts of
\qty{\NumLoShortestRigid}{\second} or longer. This depends neither on the fundamental period, uncertain
(Section~\ref{sec:lodewijks}), nor on the linear belt, strained on this fabric belt (A3, A4). The
dynamic increment does: with $\tper{1}=\qty{\NumLoPeriod}{\second}$
the start lasts \NumPracticeLo\ periods (\NumLoPracticeLow\ at the weakest coupling), falls in the
overshoot of the universal curve and needs \qty{\NumLoTtReq}{\kilo\newton}
(+\qty{\NumLoDynamicIncrement}{\percent}). With one drive pulley the grip, not the dynamic
amplification, fixes the shortest admissible start, \qty{\NumLoShortest}{\second}
(\NumLoShortestOverPeriod\ periods).

On the \qty{3}{\kilo\metre} incline with a tandem drive of \citet{suchorab2025longdistance}, the take-up near the high end
must hold the weight of the return strand down the slope, and moving it to the tail changes the
peak entry tension by less than one per cent. On the \qty{805}{\metre} conveyor of
\citet{surtees1995runback}, with the drive on the return strand, a take-up on the tight side of
the drive would need \NumSuTightFactor\ times the tension required at its real position. In all
three the take-up position acts mainly through the statics, the resistance of the return run
between drive and take-up and the weight of the return strand on a slope; its dynamic effect, a fundamental period that changes by \NumLoPeriodChange\ to
\qty{\NumSMPeriodChange}{\percent} between the real position and the tail (Lo and SM), enters through the
start time.
