In the free-end limit the spectrum of the loop is the union of the spectra of strands A
and~B (Section~\ref{sec:freeend}), and a finite take-up mass shifts each strand frequency
by the correction of Section~\ref{sec:masscorrection}. The modes are therefore followed by
strand rather than in the order of the loop: B1, B2,~\dots\ are the modes born at the
fixed--free frequencies of strand~B, and A1, A2,~\dots\ those born at the frequencies of
strand~A. In the order of the loop the second mode changes strand where A1 overtakes B2
($\xi=1/2$ for $\gamma=1$, closer to the tail for larger $\gamma$), and its frequency and
participation jump there; followed by strand, they vary smoothly and most results are in
closed form. \Cref{fig:modal} maps the fundamental in the plane $(\xi,\gamma)$, the two
parameters left when $\beta\to0$, with the conveyors of \cref{tab:cases} marked on it.

\paragraph{The fundamental belongs to strand B.} With a head drive, strand~B is the
return belt between the take-up and the tail followed by the whole carry strand, which
ends at the fixed face of the drive. For $\gamma\ge1$ it holds the fundamental for every
take-up position. In Eq.~\eqref{eq:strandB} both tangents grow from zero on
$0<\Omega<\pi/(2\gamma)$, the first pole of $\tan\gamma\Omega$, so for $\xi<1$ the product
crosses $\gamma$ once in that interval; strand~A, a uniform piece of return belt of length
$\xi\le1$, starts at $\pi/(2\xi)\ge\pi/(2\gamma)$. At $\Omega=\pi/(2\tauB)$, where
$\tauB=1-\xi+\gamma$ is the transit time of strand~B, the two arguments add up to $\pi/2$
and the product equals $1\le\gamma$, so the root lies above this frequency. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bounds_head}
  4\gamma<\tauper{1}\le4\tauB ,
\end{equation}
with equality on the right for $\gamma=1$, where strand~B is a uniform string, and in the
limit $\xi\to1$, where it is the carry strand alone. Over $1\le\gamma\le3$ the ratio
$\tauper{1}/(4\tauB)$ stays above \NumModalFourTransitMin\ (\cref{fig:modal}b): the
rule of four transits overestimates the fundamental period by up to
\qty{\NumModalFourTransitOver}{\percent}, most with the take-up next to the drive and a
large wave-speed ratio. The light return belt at the free end of strand~B raises its
frequency above that of a uniform string with the same transit time; the effect is one of
impedance, and vanishes for $\gamma=1$.

Moving the take-up changes the strand that holds the fundamental, and with it the period
(\cref{fig:modal}a). From the head to the tail the fundamental period falls by a factor
\NumHeadTailRatioGammaOne\ for $\gamma=1$ (from $8L/c_r$ to $4L/c_r$) and
\NumHeadTailRatioGammaTwo\ for $\gamma=2$; the larger $\gamma$, the more the carry strand
dominates strand~B wherever the take-up is. For the conveyor of \citet{song2012}, modelled
there with the take-up at the tail, the fundamental period is
\qty{\NumSongPeriodTailLight}{\second}; with the take-up next to the head drive, where
their Fig.~1 places it, it is \qty{\NumSongPeriodHeadLight}{\second}, and four transits of
strand~B would give \qty{\NumSongFourTransit}{\second}
($\tauper{1}/(4\tauB)=\NumSongFourTransitRatio$). Relocating the take-up is not a neutral
simplification of the model.

\paragraph{Modal participation.} By Eq.~\eqref{eq:parseval}, $\Gamma_k^2m_k/(1+\gamma^2)$ is
the fraction of the belt inertia that the start-up excites in mode~$k$. A uniform
fixed--free string has $8/\pi^2$ of its mass in its fundamental. Applied to strand~B,
whose mass is $m_B=\mu_rL\,(1-\xi+\gamma^2)$, this gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:F1}
  \frac{\Gamma_1^2m_1}{1+\gamma^2}\simeq\frac{8}{\pi^2}\,\frac{1-\xi+\gamma^2}{1+\gamma^2},
\end{equation}
exact for $\gamma=1$ and at most \qty{\NumModalParticipationFormulaErr}{\percent} too high
otherwise. The fraction ranges from \NumModalParticipationMin\ to
\NumModalParticipationMax\ (\cref{fig:modal}c) and falls as the take-up moves towards the
tail, because strand~A takes a growing share; since strand~A is uniform, A1 carries exactly
$(8/\pi^2)\,\xi/(1+\gamma^2)$. The take-up mass redistributes participation as it shifts
frequencies: written as a $2\times2$ eigenproblem, the two-pole model~\eqref{eq:twopole}
gives effective masses as well. For $\beta=0.1$ the participation of the fundamental changes
by more than \qty{5}{\percent} only in the hatched corner of \cref{fig:modal}c, at low
$\gamma$ with the take-up towards the tail, where B1 and A1 are close and the mass transfers
participation from one to the other while the pair keeps its sum; the median change over the
plane is \qty{\NumModalHatchedMedian}{\percent}.

\paragraph{Take-up at the tail of a uniform loop.} For $\gamma=1$ and $\xi=1$ the two
strands are identical, and the inertial load of the start lengthens strand~B as much as it
shortens strand~A: the quasi-static take-up travel,
$\tfrac12(\tfrac32-2\xi+\tfrac12\gamma^2)\hat a$, vanishes. The load therefore does no work
on the family $\Omega\tan\Omega=2/\beta$ of Section~\ref{sec:properties}, in whose modes
both strands lengthen together and the take-up moves: their participation factors are zero,
the start excites only the family $\cos\Omega=0$, which leaves the take-up at rest, and the
response to the drive acceleration does not depend on~$\beta$. The lowest root of the loop
is then a mode that the start does not excite. In the single-span formulation of
\citet{pang2015}, applied to a tail take-up by \citet{li2018}, the end mass shares the
rigid-body acceleration of the belt, so the inertial load does act on it and excites that
family; a take-up pulley, around which the belt runs, is not moved by the rigid motion~$U$
(Section~\ref{sec:increment}). The two results differ in their physical assumption about
the mass, not in their solution.

\paragraph{Drive on the return strand.} With the drive at $\sigma_d$ on the return strand
and the take-up downstream of it, strand~B runs from the take-up through a return run
$\ell_2$ to the tail, the whole carry strand, and a return run $\ell_1=\sigma_d/L$ from the
head to the drive; strand~A is the uniform return run between the drive and the take-up.
Its frequencies follow from
\begin{equation}\label{eq:strandB3}
  \gamma\tan\Omega\ell_1\,\tan\gamma\Omega+\tan\Omega\ell_1\,\tan\Omega\ell_2
  +\frac{1}{\gamma}\tan\Omega\ell_2\,\tan\gamma\Omega=1,
\end{equation}
which reduces to Eq.~\eqref{eq:strandB} for $\ell_1=0$, to $\tan\Omega\ell_1\tan\gamma\Omega=1/\gamma$
for a take-up at the tail ($\ell_2=0$), and, for $\gamma=1$, to $\Omega(\ell_1+1+\ell_2)=\pi/2$
by the tangent addition formula. Strand~B again holds the fundamental for $\gamma\ge1$ and
any position of the drive and take-up on the return strand: by Rayleigh's principle,
adding belt at either end of the carry strand lowers its frequency, so
$\Omega_{B1}\le\pi/(2\gamma)\le\pi/2<\Omega_{A1}$. A take-up upstream of the drive, on its
tight side, gives the same family with the roles of $\ell_1$ and $\ell_2$ exchanged, since
reversing the direction of travel leaves the spectrum unchanged; that position is
impractical for other reasons (Section~\ref{sec:validity}). The period bounds become
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bounds_intermediate}
  4\gamma<\tauper{1}\le4\tauB+4(\gamma-1)\,\ell_1,\qquad \tauB=\gamma+\ell_1+\ell_2,
\end{equation}
where the upper bound follows from giving the run $\ell_1$ the density of the carry strand,
which can only lengthen the period, and applying~\eqref{eq:bounds_head}. The run $\ell_1$
sits at the fixed end of the heavy strand and acts as a soft spring: the period now exceeds
four transits, by up to \qty{\NumIntermediateOverMax}{\percent} for $\gamma=3$
(\cref{fig:modal}d), and the rule of four transits errs in opposite directions with a head
and with an intermediate drive. For $\gamma=1$ only the total length of the strand matters.
On the geometry of \citet{nordell1984}, with the drive \qty{5150}{ft} from the head and the
take-up \qty{300}{ft} downstream of it, the fundamental period is
\qty{\NumNCPeriod}{\second} against four transits of \qty{\NumNCFourTransit}{\second}
($\tauper{1}/(4\tauB)=\NumNCRatio$); with a head drive and the same strand lengths it would be
\qty{\NumNCPeriodHead}{\second} ($\NumNCRatioHead$), so the position of the drive alone
lengthens the period by \qty{\NumNCIncrease}{\percent}. Since the modes of a strand share out
its mass, the effective mass of the fundamental never exceeds $m_B$. Moving the drive away
from the head thus lengthens $\tper{1}$ and calls for slower starts at the same dynamic
amplification (Section~\ref{sec:startup}).

\paragraph{The threshold.} Let $\Omega_p$ be a fixed--free frequency of one strand and
$\Omega_t=\Omega_p/1.05$ the frequency of the same mode with a period \qty{5}{\percent}
longer. Since the characteristic equation in the form~\eqref{eq:receptance} is linear in
$1/\beta$, the take-up mass that places a root at $\Omega_t$ is explicit,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:beta5}
  \betafive=\frac{4}{\Omega_t^2\,\bigl[H_A(\Omega_t)+H_B(\Omega_t)\bigr]},
\end{equation}
and by interlacing that root is the one born at $\Omega_p$, provided no frequency of either
strand lies in $[\Omega_t,\Omega_p)$. Where one does, the modes of the two strands veer and
the shift is not a property of one strand; where $H_A+H_B\le0$ at $\Omega_t$, the
\qty{5}{\percent} shift is not reached even for $\beta\to\infty$, the limit of a fixed
take-up pulley. For an isolated pole the one-pole formula~\eqref{eq:onepole} gives
$\betafive\simeq4\tilde m(1.05^2-1)=\NumSinglePoleCoefficient\,\tilde m$.

For the fundamental (\cref{fig:beta}a), $\betafive$ ranges from \NumBetaFiveBOneMin\ to
\NumBetaFiveBOneMax\ (median \NumBetaFiveBOneMedian) and depends mainly on $\gamma$: the
heavier the carry strand, the larger the mass that strand~B can carry at its free end. The
minimum lies at the tail with $\gamma=1$, where B1 and A1 coincide and the take-up mass
acts on both. For $\gamma\ge1.4$ and the take-up within $\xi\le0.1$ of the drive,
$\betafive\ge\NumBetaFiveMinNearDrive$; for every take-up position, $\betafive\ge0.5$ needs
$\gamma\ge\NumRuleGammaAnyPosition$, and at the tail with $\gamma=1.4$ the threshold falls
to \NumBetaFiveMinGammaOneFour. The second mode of strand~B is more sensitive (median
\NumBetaFiveBTwoMedian, \cref{fig:beta}b).

Strand~A is uniform, so the single-pole estimate with $\tilde m=\xi/2$ gives
$\betafive(\mathrm{A1})\simeq\NumBetaFiveAOneSlope\,\xi$ (\cref{fig:beta}c): a small
threshold, but for a mode that matters little. A1 carries a fraction $(8/\pi^2)\,\xi/(1+\gamma^2)$
of the belt inertia; its damping ratio under the Kelvin--Voigt law,
$\zeta_{A1}=\zeta_1\tauper{1}/(4\xi)$, grows as the take-up approaches the drive; and its
period, $4\xi L/c_r$, is short against every published start: even with the take-up at the
tail, the starts of the cases in \cref{tab:cases} last at least \NumAOneStartRatioMin\ periods
of A1 (Lo), so they drive it quasi-statically. A1 matters only with the take-up near the
tail and a fast start. In the one case with the take-up at the tail, St, the take-up mass
lengthens A1 by \qty{\NumStAOneShift}{\percent}.

\paragraph{Where the conveyors lie.} By Eq.~\eqref{eq:beta_tension}, the mass ratio follows
from the take-up tension, $\beta=(4/\nstr)\lambda\,\Tt/\Wr$ with $\Wr=g\mu_rL$, and can be
factorised as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:beta_factors}
  \beta=\frac{4\lambda}{\nstr}\,\frac{F_U}{\Wr}\,\frac{\Tt}{F_U},
\end{equation}
where $F_U$ is the running peripheral force at the drive. For a given slope, load and
resistance coefficients, neither $F_U/\Wr$ nor $\Tt/F_U$ depends on the length: the take-up
tension is sized by requirements that grow in proportion to the length, chiefly the grip at
the drive during running and start-up \citep[Eqs.~47--50]{din22101} and the weight of the
return strand on a slope, with the sag limits \citep[Eqs.~51--52]{din22101} as a further
floor, or by other criteria such as holdback or braking. The last column of
\cref{tab:cases} gives the criterion stated or implied by each source. The mass ratio of a
gravity take-up therefore does not decrease with the length of the conveyor. The values of
$\beta$ in \cref{tab:cases} are computed from the design take-up tension of each source,
which can be checked against its static balance, rather than from a published counterweight
mass.

For the conveyors of \qty{1}{\kilo\metre} or longer in \cref{tab:cases}, $\Tt/\Wr$ is
\NumLongTtWrMin\ to \NumLongTtWrMax, $\beta\le\NumLongBetaMax$ and $\beta/\betafive$ of the
fundamental is \NumLongBetaRatioMin\ to \NumLongBetaRatioMax: they lie at least
\NumLongMarginMin\ times below their threshold, \NumLongMarginMinLow\ times at the weakest
coupling of their material. The take-up mass lengthens their fundamental period by at most
\qty{\NumLongShiftMax}{\percent}, and by at most \qty{\NumLongShiftMaxLow}{\percent} over the
coupling range of each material. With a counterweight hung directly on two strands,
$\beta=2\Tt/\Wr$; for a take-up near the drive and $\gamma\ge1.4$ the threshold of the
fundamental is at least \NumBetaFiveMinNearDrive, so the take-up mass changes the
fundamental by less than \qty{5}{\percent} as long as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rule}
  \Tt\lesssim\frac{\Wr}{4}.
\end{equation}
This gives a dynamic meaning to the long conveyor, a term without a strict definition in
the technical literature \citep{suchorab2025longdistance}: a conveyor is long, for its
longitudinal dynamics, when the take-up tension is small against the weight of the return
strand, $\beta\ll\betafive$, so that the take-up acts as a free end and its mass enters only
through the correction of Section~\ref{sec:masscorrection}. Multiple reeving moves a take-up
further into this regime, since $\beta$ scales as $1/\nstr$ at the same tension
(Section~\ref{sec:nstrands}).

In short conveyors the floors that do not scale with the length, sag and holdback, set the
take-up tension, and $\beta$ grows as $1/L$. The conveyor of \citet{wheatley2021}
(\qty{275}{\metre}, $\beta=\NumWRBeta$, take-up position not published) lies at
\NumWRRatioMin--\NumWRRatioMax\ of its threshold for any take-up position, and at
\NumWRRatioLowMin--\NumWRRatioLowMax\ at the weakest coupling of its material, where the
take-up mass lengthens its fundamental by \qtyrange{\NumWRShiftLowMin}{\NumWRShiftLowMax}{\percent}.
Surtees' conveyor (\qty{805}{\metre}, drive on the return strand, $\beta=\NumSuBeta$) lies
at \NumSuRatio\ of its threshold (\NumSuRatioLow\ at the weakest coupling), with a
lengthening of \qty{\NumSuShift}{\percent} (\qty{\NumSuShiftLow}{\percent}). The two shorter
conveyors of the plant of \citet{wheatley2021}, of \qty{\NumWRBLength}{\metre} and
\qty{\NumWRCLength}{\metre}, have published counterweights of \num{10} and \qty{6}{\tonne}; with two
strands and a take-up near the drive assumed, $\beta=\NumWRBBeta$ and \NumWRCBeta\ and
$\beta/\betafive=\NumWRBRatio$ and \NumWRCRatio: the shortest is the only conveyor of the set
beyond the threshold. In these conveyors the exact characteristic
equation~\eqref{eq:chareq} keeps the take-up mass; in long conveyors the free-end spectra of
Section~\ref{sec:frequencies}, with the closed-form correction, suffice, which is also why
none of the field records of Section~\ref{sec:validation} can test the take-up mass.
